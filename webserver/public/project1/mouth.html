MOUTH

The mouth is meant to do two things: taste, and speak.
It usually only manages one.
Some tastes are bitter, but what comes out when I speak is sweet — like "I'm fine," "it's good," "don't worry about me."
The real taste stays at the back of the tongue, in the part that never got said.
What I love most is spicy, heavy-flavored food
maybe because my hometown is wall-to-wall hot pot, or maybe it's the humid, muggy weather. Anything too bland is hard to swallow
and so is a life that's too bland. My weak stomach needs some kind of jolt before it can properly break things down.
I'm greedy for that rush of eating, so much so that in my first year abroad
nowhere felt right, I couldn't sleep so I started eating too much.
Thinking the prices and exchange rate were so high, I told myself I had to finish everything, so I took more than I could hold, and stuffed in more than I could actually eat.
What's yours stays yours
you can always eat it later.
There's no need to cram in more than you can digest just because you're afraid of scarcity in the moment.
Life works the same way.
