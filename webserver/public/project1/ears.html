EARS

Some sounds I kept, most I didn't.
What stays is rarely the loudest?
a sentence that stops halfway through, a car passing beneath my window late at night and then fading away,
the beep-beep of the street, the sound of a machine cutting through wood.
Ears can't close, so they remember for me many things I meant to forget.
On ordinary days, different melodies and lyrics surface in my head
some trending, some cheerful, and some whose names I've long forgotten.
I love ASMR, the way it sounds like a hand massaging the brain, easing you into a rhythm.
I also love the sound of a singing bowl struck since it's the tremor it leaves behind that I'm drawn to.
I love the sound of running water too
— that rushing, splashing wash of it. When spring arrives, the sound of things growing is thunderous;
and now, with autumn almost here, I can already hear the crisp crunch of footsteps on fallen leaves.
The instant my family's voices come through the phone, I feel wrapped up, held steady.
I listen to their rambling reminders, feeling both the plainness and the truth of living alone
the clatter of dishes and a cutting board washed, and sometimes dropped by accident.
I listen to the sound of air blowing from the AC
proof that everything is still running. The echo goes on, unbroken.
My echo goes on, unbroken.
